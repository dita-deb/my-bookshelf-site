My Thoughts

I truly did not expect Assistant to the Villain to blow me away the way it did. The comedy is very tongue-in-cheek, and I genuinely had a good time with it. Most of the jokes were fun, and even when they didn’t fully land, they still earned a smile or scoff through sheer cringe, corniness, or deliberate stupidity.

That said, the secondhand embarrassment at the beginning was rough. I side-eyed nearly every decision Evie Sage made. This girl was THIRSTING for The Villain when she and we readers practically knew nothing about him, including his name. The starting humor is kind of dirty-minded humor, but honestly, it’s still okay (barely) because it's all taken as lighthearted nonsense (because our gal Evie Sage just loves rambling). But slowly, very slowly, her infectious, cheery nature and earnestness won me over. I was concerned at my first impressions of her when she kinda shrugged off harassment, abuse, and sexism for the longest time. I’m looking back at the comments and highlights of the book, and I definitely started liking Evie around chapter 20. By the end, I could genuinely see myself being her friend if I lived in this world, which is wild now that I see all the harsh comments and judgment I was giving her in the first 20 chapters.

The Villain himself was interesting—but he wasn’t actually a villain. He was dark and broody with banter and sarcasm to boot, but it just wasn’t villainous enough. I really wish his backstory had been revealed much earlier, because learning it roughly five percent from the end made it difficult to fully understand his role in the story, the plot, and his relationships with other characters. (I watched a book review with The Book Leo and she’s so real for saying that he’s literally a badass like Uncle Iroh escaping the way he did). I didn’t think too much into how he monologued his past majority of the time, but it's kind of a cop out in a way to chop up his character like that to keep character moments short (the book was 60-some chapters, it's okay to have more pages describing characters more with more to them). His reactions and inner thoughts in response to Evie’s chaos were endlessly entertaining, but despite the title, he never actively commits any crimes within the book. Everything is framed as an action fantasy unfolding largely within an office setting—and I’ll admit, office romances are a soft spot for me, even though I don’t indulge in them often.

What truly sold me, however, was the restraint in Evie and the Villain’s relationship. The chaste, slow-burn affection they share—right up until the final moments—made every interaction feel intentional and meaningful. And the ending? I was outraged to the point of tears. I will be reading book two. That is a promise I’m holding myself to.

This book is very plot-driven, and the characters genuinely cannot catch a break—something is always happening, which I’m not mad about. It keeps my mind busy trying to juggle all the things going on as an overarching story takes place in the forefront. The mystery surrounding the mole in the office was a major highlight for me. I was pointing fingers just as wildly as Evie, if not more. The reveal had me ready to throw hands on her behalf.

I also appreciated how the book handled trauma. It’s approached with care and respect, without forcing emotional confessions just for dramatic payoff. Evie holds back until she’s ready, and when she finally opens up, it doesn’t feel rushed or pressured. The Villain—despite the title—is calm, attentive, and genuinely listens before reacting. He isn’t a cookie-cutter villain, but I wouldn’t call him deeply written either; much of his internal conflict comes from suppressing his affection for Evie and prioritizing revenge. Book two better not give me the “obvious ending”—I want the true villain exposed and our “Villain” to earn his redemption.

And I have to mention the two scenes that had me absolutely giggling. The goofy cover-up story Evie made to get everyone to safety had me dying. I was already smiling and laughing at her acting as the dramatic wife to the Villain, as he lay unconscious. But then he was starting to wake up and she's thinking “Frick he's gonna wake up and murder everyone, I need to shut him up now-” and kisses him without hesitation. THIS MAN LEGIT THOUGHT IT WAS A DREAM and CONTINUED TO MAKE OUT PUBLICLY INFRONT OF STANGERS, HIS FAMILY, AND FRIENDS. The way I started cackling and tearing up from how publicly this man was enamored by Evie, and she was just like “oh thank gods you heard the made-up story I gave the knights to let us go.” He's shocked, going a 100 miles a minute, letting his soul catch up to how he just made out with Evie and called her his love in public with everyone to see. It was, in fact, not a dream at all.

The second was the hug they shared after the mole was revealed, which had my heart racing. It was deeply intimate without being smutty—just pure yearning. And I am an absolute sucker for yearning.

I really liked this book, but I can see how it might not be for everyone, especially because you’d have to tough it out for 25% of the book and be blind to its shortcomings in character depth.
