My Thoughts

Emily Wilde’s Map of the Otherlands was just as fantastical as the first book, though notably less cozy in atmosphere. The moments of humor and calm between Emily and Wendell were present, but far more sparingly. The overall story had a sharper sense of tension and urgency which makes sense because poor Wendell got poisoned on his birthday of all days (no this isnt a spoiler it happens within the first two chapters).

Emily continues to be the novel’s greatest strength. Her intelligence and resourcefulness are repeatedly emphasized, especially in contrast to Rose, whose role as both antagonist and uneasy ally highlights how consistently capable Emily is. I did wish Ariadne had been more involved, but her limited role ultimately made sense—she remains too young to be pivotal until her fresh perspective becomes necessary to move the plot forward.

The underlying lore surrounding the missing scholars, De Grey and Eichorn, was intriguing, but it ultimately became my greatest frustration with the book. By the end, they felt more like the fae who tormented them than the scholars they once were. I had hoped they would represent a breakthrough—answers, clarity, or even active resistance—but instead we relied solely on Emily to figure it out by herself. She is brilliant, but I find it a tiny bit of stretch for her to solve a decades-old mystery in a couple of weeks. While this reinforced Emily’s strength as a protagonist, it made their long-anticipated presence feel underwhelming.

Speaking of underwhelming, I had not expected Wendell’s stepmother to be that careless especially after giving her grand speech of mortals being the dangerous ones despite Faeries having such power, numbers and strength. That whole interaction was well meaning to show how prepared Emily was, but still.. Maybe as a reader I had expected a longer fight between two mortals involved in Faerie court systems.

That said, the novel does an excellent job of setting the stage for what’s to come. With war looming and the next book poised to explore a fight for the throne, I’m eager to see how the suspicious “saviors” from the Summer Court will help—or harm—our protagonists when the stakes are no longer purely academic, but political and deadly.

While less cozy than its predecessor, Map of the Otherlands expands the world, sharpens its conflicts, and leaves the series on a compelling precipice that makes the next installment impossible not to anticipate.
