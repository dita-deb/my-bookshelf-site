My Thoughts

I picked up Apprentice to the Villain immediately after finishing [b:Assistant to the Villain|123257687|Assistant to the Villain (Assistant to the Villain, #1)|Hannah Nicole Maehrer|https://i.gr-assets.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1702057336l/123257687._SY75_.jpg|144626864], and I knew exactly what I was getting into. The book definitely maintained the tone and charm of the first installment, and I was happy to spend more time with the side characters as their secrets slowly came to light. I like the small friendship between Blade and Trystan forming while Becky and Evie were starting to patch up their differences, too. That said, I couldn’t shake the feeling that there was a lot of missed potential. While romance was in the air, disaster was too, starting with too many things happening to the characters rather than happening because of them. Consequences felt light, and progression often came too easily, even when obstacles were introduced.

That issue became clearer with the book’s increasing reliance on destiny. Evie getting the job, surviving the death-fruit—both are retroactively framed as destiny’s interference rather than earned outcomes. By the epilogue, destiny is positioned as the driving force behind not just past events, but future ones as well. Gideon’s belief that the Story of Rennedawn may point to Evie as the unmasked Villain reframes the prophecy entirely, while the Creature of Destiny’s warning—that Trystan would be her undoing and she his making—pushes Trystan into the conclusion that separation is the only way to protect her but that may not mean anything if she's the prophesized villain. The result is a narrative where inevitability starts to overshadow agency, and character decisions feel less like choices and more like reactions to prophecy.

The pining between Trystan and Evie initially worked for me, but the prolonged "will-they-won't-they" separation started to wear thin. At a certain point, the “unrequited love” angle felt unbelievable—everyone around them could see they were clearly in love except for the two of them. It crossed from yearning into outright delusion. Evie, whom I previously admired for her communication, seemed to lose that strength entirely in this book, which was frustrating. Additionally, I also always saw Evie in a bloodthirsty type light, so her sudden bloodlust wasn't shocking to me as it seems to be to the others. I'm just holding out on her messing with Trystan in the next book to the point he has to admit to her he's in love.

Okay, but some of the iconic moments between Evie and Trystan that happened in this book still have me in a complete chokehold well after the ending. I can’t even believe I’m saying this, but Trystan is a massive green flag when it comes to consent. His vigilance around it genuinely made me feral in the best and worst way. The fact that he refused to kiss or take advantage of Evie when she was drunk—even when it felt like they might be facing their last moments—had me fuming and giggling through actual tears. It was infuriating. It was tender. It was everything. This sentiment is truly what carried me through the book.

Despite the irritation, the angst still hit hard. Some moments were truly heart-wrenching, even if a few pushed my patience to its limits. And honestly? I enjoyed the suffering enough that I’ll absolutely be picking up the next book. Flaws and all, I’m still invested.
