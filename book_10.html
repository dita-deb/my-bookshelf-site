My Thoughts

I’m genuinely conflicted about how to rate this one because it was better than [b:Falling|240583638|Falling|Christina Lauren|https://i.gr-assets.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1769514015l/240583638._SX50_.jpg|258647220], but not more than [b:Hot for Slayer|240581511|Hot for Slayer|Ali Hazelwood|https://i.gr-assets.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1756061884l/240581511._SY75_.jpg|258642212]. Maybe I'll have to go back and make adjustments to my ratings later as I see fit.

On one hand, I liked the story itself. The lore behind the curse was intriguing, and the eventual resolution felt thoughtful and emotionally grounded. I loved the world-building too, as it truly felt like the world of voodoo and dark magic, similar to The Princess and the Frog movie.

Dax fits squarely into the brooding, moody male archetype (which is to say he is quite basic despite his magically inked body)—burdened with a curse that causes any woman who falls in love with him to die. It’s dramatic, tragic, and very Halloween-coded. That part is fine.

Petra, however, was more complicated. I didn’t love the term of endearment “Notebook” (a nod to her being a journalist). It never quite landed as charming and instead felt awkward. I also struggled with her pushy nature at times; it wasn't charming in the slightest, seeing her cross his boundaries like it's nothing. That said, their banter was genuinely enjoyable—sharp, quick, and often the highlight of their dynamic. When they were simply talking, the chemistry felt fun.

My larger issue is structural.

This series consists of short Halloween-themed standalones written by different authors, each set in entirely separate worlds. They are not meant to overlap or build a shared universe. So it was frustrating that this story dropped hints of something much bigger—threads that felt like the start of a broader mythology—without resolving them. It ends with unanswered cliffhangers that suggest an expanding universe… but this is the universe. There’s nowhere else for those threads to go (unless I'm missing the memo that this story has a follow-up somewhere floating in the ether).

When I finished it, I thought it was fine—solid, entertaining, and resolved enough. But sitting with it a day later, the open-ended implications don’t feel satisfying. They feel incomplete.

I enjoyed the curse lore. I liked the central premise. I appreciated the banter. But the tonal and structural choices—especially for what’s supposed to be a contained standalone—leave me uncertain.

So for now, it sits somewhere in the middle. Not a miss, but not okay.
