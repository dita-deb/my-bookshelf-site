My Thoughts

I fell in love with the way this book is written—the way the words flow so naturally they feel less like prose and more like a quiet conversation between me and the page. There’s an intimacy to the narration that makes you forget you’re reading at all; instead, you’re listening, remembering, feeling alongside it.

I adored how the story is slowly chipped away and revealed, how moments echo across chapters, resurfacing later with deeper meaning. It’s the kind of storytelling that trusts the reader—to notice, to remember, to feel. I don’t even think I’m competent enough to fully articulate how emotional this book made me, or how deeply enamored I became with those teenagers on the pier, with Louisa and Ted, as their lives stretched across time and distance.

Reading [b:My Friends|217163697|My Friends|Fredrik Backman|https://i.gr-assets.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1734625930l/217163697._SY75_.jpg|223642719] felt like being wrapped in a hug from my childhood. I craved this book like a swim on a hot summer day, a cold soda after a run, and a bedtime story under cozy, soft sheets. How the hell did I go my entire life without reading this masterpiece?

The way these people loved and lived was both awe-inspiring and devastatingly beautiful. I cried constantly while reading—grieving and cherishing the small, precious memories that stayed with Ted throughout his life: the bird they saved, the shopping cart rides, the art competition used as a distraction, and swimming in the sea. I felt Joar’s pain so sharply—the quiet devastation of being beaten down by a bitter, cripple-minded old man who projected his own insecurity and unhappiness onto a child. I ached over the way Ali chose to trust and believe in the boys after everything that had been done to her. I mourned alongside Ted and C. Jat, whose grief wasn’t physical but mental—a loss that consumed them through silence, memory, and distance. The emotional blocks and barriers these teens endured felt heartbreakingly real. I was right beside Lousia as we experienced everything in those moments that had already happened in our present together.

I completely broke down at the end, realizing I wouldn’t get to see them all grow old together, still smiling. I was right beside Lousia the entire time.

What I’m struggling to explain—what feels almost impossible to put into words—is that my heart hadn’t experienced this kind of feeling from a book before. The closest I can come is this: there is a life before My Friends, and now there is a life after it. And I don’t know how to endure the after without carrying this story with me.

I simply couldn’t put it down, and hands down, it will probably be my favorite book of the year.
