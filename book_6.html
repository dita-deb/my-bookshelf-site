My Thoughts

I knew how this would end. We were warned in the synopsis and again in the opening chapters. And still, when it finally happened, it shocked me and left me heartbroken.

Watching Jet fall apart—even as she kept reminding herself and us that she was dying—was devastating. The moment that hurt the most was her final realization about Billy: initially suspecting him, knowing deep down he wasn’t the one, and choosing peace over answers. He wanted to tell her something just so she could have closure, but Jet was already complete by then. That unrequited love between them made me bitter in the way only a hopeless romantic can be. She knew it wasn’t in their stars and wanted him to find new ones—and that acceptance hurt more than denial ever could.

Billy ended up being the emotional backbone of the story, and I regret my early impressions of him (a nerdy, quiet guy just trying to shoot his shot). I adored his own silent grieving and bonding with Jet till the end. Jet’s dark, copium humor pulled me in immediately, and I found myself relating to her more than I expected—the belief that there’s always more time, the way commitments get postponed, the restless search for something to do. I felt miserably hopeless alongside her, and it made her final days—so productive, so honest—feel both refreshing and unfair.

Her letters absolutely destroyed me. I truly cried the most when I read her handwritten letters for her family and loved ones. It tied up her story neatly, though not all answers would be given by the end of the book.

The ending is one I both love and hate. I hated the funeral. I hated that there was a funeral at all. I hated the small, irrational part of me that hoped for a miracle. But the more I sat with it, the more it felt inevitable—something Jet herself seemed to understand. Her heartbreak wasn’t just for herself, but for Billy and the ending of a story they never truly got to live.

I didn’t love how everything "resolved" at first, especially the final act of violence, but with time I appreciated the sense of consequence. I needed to let my mind stew on the consequences and climax at the end of the story, it truly went to shit there Billy... No one escapes untouched.
